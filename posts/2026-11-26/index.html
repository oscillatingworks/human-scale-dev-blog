26 November 2026 · Today in the city

Estates get lawns and benches, and corners get street signs

Three changes you can see today. One kerb corner gave us trouble.

Estates with grass, trees and benches

The big estates of slabs and towers now sit in open green land. We add lawns, paths and trees in loose groups. Benches stand along the paths.

Slab and tower doors get a glass canopy. The slabs and towers get balconies. Tall buildings show a lift room and roof equipment on top.

Why does this matter? Open land with a bench and a tree gives people a place to stop. A bare estate makes people walk fast. A soft edge makes them stay.

Street name signs at corners

Street name signs now hang at junction corners. Each street name appears once per corner. A sign never covers another sign.

Why does this matter? On foot, you decide at corners. A name on the corner helps you choose a way. Corners are also where short blocks give you more routes.

Large stores sell different goods on each row

In the supermarket and the market hall, the rows no longer look the same. Each shelf row has its own goods. Each market stall has its own trade.

The challenge: a kerb corner that leaned into the road

At a junction, the round kerb corners can look wrong when a street bends at the junction. The corner tilted into the paving.

We fixed it. Now each corner follows the kerb of each street where the corner meets it.

That is the day in the city.
