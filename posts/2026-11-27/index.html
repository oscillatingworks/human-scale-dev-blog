People open umbrellas when it rains

Today the city gets weather, and the people in it notice.

Dressed for the day

Each city now has a climate of its own. The day has four weather blocks, with a temperature that rises and falls, and snow and puddles on the ground. The sound follows too. Birds go quiet in heavy rain.

The people on your street react. Shorts become trousers in the cold, and a coat comes out when it freezes. Not everyone feels the cold in the same way, so two neighbours can dress differently on the same morning. In rain, people open an umbrella or pull up a hood.

Some people skip their usual walk outside when it is wet, snowy or very hot. Your phone shows the weather and the forecast.

A chair by the door

Home doors and walls now have small things next to them. We add door chairs, plant pots and wall benches, and which ones you see depends on the character of the neighbourhood. They sit on the front strip or on the paving, and the walk stays clear.

Why does this matter? Where a building meets the street, a soft edge makes people slow down and stay. A bench or a pot of plants gives a reason to stop. A long blank wall does the opposite, and people walk faster along it.

Crossing at the crossing

The road now stops you. You cross at crossing places, and the kerb is a real edge. At each plain crossing there is a flush strip with small dots in the paving, so you can see where to cross. Residents also cross at these places. A careful walker may even take a crossing a little out of the way.

Today’s challenge: signs that fit their street

Shops now show signs in four looks: classic, modern, minimal and old. Each district has its own mix.

To do this, every shop has to know which street it faces. That work made the game stutter while you walked. We changed how shops find their street, and the walk is smooth again. In nearly all cases the sign now matches the street it faces.
